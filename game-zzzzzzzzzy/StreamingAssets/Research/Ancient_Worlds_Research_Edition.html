The approved direction

Deep coverage · approximately 85%

Egypt, Nubia, Cyprus, Phoenicia and the entire Levantine corridor—including smaller cities and city-states—join the original Mesopotamian, Anatolian, Iranian, Arabian, Afghan and Turkmenistan coverage.

Wider context · approximately 15%

Selected Mediterranean, European, northern Asian, Chinese, South Asian and Southeast Asian histories. Lighter research density and terrain detail; the same sourcing standard.

One connected historical record

A person, place or event can be explored from several views. Corrections belong to the underlying record so later maps, timelines and dynasty views can remain consistent.

Coverage is distinct from certainty. The 85/15 target allocates attention and content. It is not a confidence score, a population estimate or a division of land area.

Map and visual decisions

  • Clear reconstructed border lines where the evidence supports a reasonable territorial interpretation.
  • Localized blending at genuinely ambiguous frontier zones.
  • Separate markers for cities and dispersed communities.
  • A charcoal surround, a subdued outer context band and a final non-navigable shroud.
  • 91 boundary-date maps: 3200, 3170, …, 530, 500 BCE.
  • Detailed playback can stop at documented changes between those dates.

Research and execution sequence

1 · Establish the evidence

Resolve names and locations, preserve source dates, distinguish source claims from scholarly interpretations, and record disputed alternatives.

2 · Reconstruct selected chapters

Use the Hammurabi-era evidence and an Egypt-connected chapter, then contrast them with an archaeological case where political history is less complete.

3 · Extend and render

Review spatial claims before extending to all dates. Generate the snapshot atlas and engine-ready data only after territorial and landscape interpretations are documented.
