**Final core vocabulary of U.S. law terms** (~175 high-value terms, overlaps reduced, categorized intelligently) Very cursory definitions accompany each term. ### 1. Sources of Law & System Foundations 1. **Constitution** — Highest law of the land. 2. **Statute** — Law passed by a legislature. 3. **Regulation** — Rule issued by a government agency. 4. **Common law** — Judge-made law from court decisions. 5. **Case law** — Published judicial decisions. 6. **Precedent** — Earlier decision used to decide a later similar case. 7. **Binding precedent** — Precedent a court must follow. 8. **Persuasive authority** — Source a court may consider but need not follow. 9. **Stare decisis** — Doctrine of following precedent. 10. **Holding** — Binding legal rule of a case. 11. **Dicta** — Non-binding remarks in an opinion. 12. **Jurisdiction** — Court’s power to hear a case. 13. **Subject-matter jurisdiction** — Power over the type of case. 14. **Personal jurisdiction** — Power over the particular parties. 15. **Venue** — Proper geographic location for the case. 16. **Standing** — Party’s concrete stake that allows them to sue. 17. **Cause of action** — Legal claim that can support a lawsuit. 18. **Substantive law** — Law that defines rights, duties, and crimes. 19. **Procedural law** — Rules that govern how cases move through courts. 20. **Due process** — Fair procedures before government takes life, liberty, or property. ### 2. Courts & Participants 21. **Party / Litigant** — Person or entity directly involved in a case. 22. **Plaintiff** — Party who starts a civil lawsuit. 23. **Defendant** — Party sued or criminally accused. 24. **Petitioner** — Party asking a court for relief or review. 25. **Respondent** — Party opposing a petition or appeal. 26. **Prosecutor** — Government lawyer bringing criminal charges. 27. **Defense counsel** — Lawyer for the accused or civil defendant. 28. **Public defender** — Court-appointed lawyer for those who cannot afford one. 29. **Judge** — Official who presides and decides legal questions. 30. **Jury / Petit jury** — Citizens who decide facts and render a verdict. 31. **Juror** — Individual member of a jury. 32. **Grand jury** — Body that decides whether to issue an indictment. 33. **Clerk of court** — Official who manages records and filings. 34. **Bailiff** — Court officer who keeps order and handles the jury. 35. **Court reporter** — Person who creates the official transcript. 36. **Witness** — Person who gives testimony. 37. **Expert witness** — Specialist allowed to give opinion testimony. 38. **Pro se** — Representing oneself without a lawyer. 39. **Amicus curiae** — Non-party who submits argument to assist the court. 40. **Counsel / Attorney** — Licensed lawyer. ### 3. Civil Litigation Process 41. **Complaint** — Document that starts a civil lawsuit. 42. **Answer** — Defendant’s formal written response. 43. **Pleadings** — Formal written claims and defenses. 44. **Service of process** — Formal delivery of court papers. 45. **Summons** — Notice that a lawsuit has been filed. 46. **Default judgment** — Judgment entered for failure to respond. 47. **Motion** — Request asking the court for a ruling. 48. **Motion to dismiss** — Request to throw out the case for legal failure. 49. **Summary judgment** — Judgment without trial when no material facts are disputed. 50. **Brief** — Written legal argument. 51. **Discovery** — Pretrial exchange of information and evidence. 52. **Deposition** — Sworn out-of-court testimony. 53. **Interrogatories** — Written questions answered under oath. 54. **Request for production** — Demand for documents or things. 55. **Request for admission** — Request to admit or deny specific facts. 56. **Subpoena** — Court order to appear or produce evidence. 57. **Subpoena duces tecum** — Subpoena requiring production of documents. 58. **Settlement** — Agreement that ends a dispute without full trial. 59. **Mediation** — Neutral helps parties negotiate a voluntary resolution. 60. **Arbitration** — Neutral decides the dispute, often privately. 61. **Class action** — Lawsuit by representatives on behalf of a large group. 62. **Counterclaim** — Claim by defendant against plaintiff. 63. **Cross-claim** — Claim by one co-party against another. ### 4. Contracts 64. **Contract** — Legally enforceable agreement. 65. **Offer** — Proposal to enter a bargain. 66. **Acceptance** — Agreement to the terms of an offer. 67. **Consideration** — Something of value exchanged by each side. 68. **Capacity** — Legal ability to enter a contract. 69. **Mutual assent** — Agreement on the same terms. 70. **Statute of Frauds** — Rule requiring certain contracts to be in writing. 71. **Breach of contract** — Failure to perform a contractual duty. 72. **Material breach** — Serious breach that excuses the other party. 73. **UCC** — Uniform Commercial Code governing sales of goods. 74. **Warranty** — Promise about quality or title of goods. 75. **Specific performance** — Court order requiring a party to perform the contract. ### 5. Torts 76. **Tort** — Civil wrong other than breach of contract. 77. **Negligence** — Failure to use reasonable care that causes harm. 78. **Duty** — Legal obligation to act with reasonable care. 79. **Breach (of duty)** — Failure to meet the required standard of care. 80. **Causation (cause-in-fact)** — Harm would not have occurred without the conduct. 81. **Proximate cause** — Legal limit on liability based on foreseeability. 82. **Compensatory damages** — Money to make the injured party whole. 83. **Punitive damages** — Money awarded to punish and deter. 84. **Strict liability** — Liability without proof of fault. 85. **Product liability** — Liability for defective products that cause injury. 86. **Assault** — Intentional act causing fear of imminent harmful contact. 87. **Battery** — Intentional harmful or offensive contact. 88. **False imprisonment** — Intentional confinement without legal authority. 89. **Defamation** — False statement that harms reputation (libel or slander). 90. **Intentional infliction of emotional distress** — Extreme conduct causing severe emotional harm. 91. **Trespass** — Unauthorized entry onto land or interference with property. 92. **Conversion** — Serious interference with another’s personal property. 93. **Affirmative defense** — Defense that, if proved, defeats liability even if the claim elements are met. ### 6. Criminal Law & Procedure 94. **Crime** — Conduct prohibited and punishable by the government. 95. **Charge / Count** — Formal accusation of a specific offense. 96. **Arrest** — Taking a person into legal custody. 97. **Arrest warrant** — Court order authorizing an arrest. 98. **Search warrant** — Court order authorizing a search. 99. **Probable cause** — Reasonable basis to believe a crime occurred or evidence exists. 100. **Bail** — Conditions set to ensure appearance in court. 101. **Arraignment** — Hearing where charges are read and a plea is entered. 102. **Indictment** — Formal charge issued by a grand jury. 103. **Information** — Formal charge filed by a prosecutor. 104. **Plea** — Defendant’s formal response to charges. 105. **Guilty plea** — Admission of guilt. 106. **Not-guilty plea** — Denial of guilt requiring the government to prove its case. 107. **Nolo contendere** — “No contest” plea treated like a guilty plea for sentencing. 108. **Plea bargain** — Agreement to plead guilty in exchange for concessions. 109. **Acquittal** — Finding of not guilty. 110. **Conviction** — Judgment of guilt. 111. **Sentence** — Punishment imposed after conviction. 112. **Felony** — Serious crime usually punishable by more than one year in prison. 113. **Misdemeanor** — Lesser crime usually punishable by fine or jail under one year. 114. **Mens rea** — Required guilty mental state. 115. **Actus reus** — Prohibited act or omission. 116. **Intent** — Purpose to bring about a result. 117. **Recklessness** — Conscious disregard of a substantial risk. 118. **Criminal negligence** — Serious failure to perceive a substantial risk. 119. **Beyond a reasonable doubt** — Highest burden of proof, used in criminal cases. 120. **Double jeopardy** — Protection against being tried twice for the same offense. 121. **Self-incrimination** — Compelled testimony against oneself (limited by Fifth Amendment). 122. **Miranda warning** — Required warnings before custodial interrogation. 123. **Habeas corpus** — Writ challenging the legality of detention. 124. **Exclusionary rule** — Rule barring evidence obtained unconstitutionally. ### 7. Evidence & Trial Mechanics 125. **Evidence** — Information offered to prove or disprove facts. 126. **Admissible evidence** — Evidence the fact-finder is allowed to consider. 127. **Relevance** — Tendency to make a material fact more or less probable. 128. **Unfair prejudice** — Risk that evidence will lead to an improper decision. 129. **Objection** — Challenge to a question, answer, or exhibit. 130. **Sustained** — Judge agrees with the objection. 131. **Overruled** — Judge rejects the objection. 132. **Hearsay** — Out-of-court statement offered for its truth (generally excluded). 133. **Hearsay exception** — Rule allowing certain hearsay. 134. **Direct evidence** — Evidence that proves a fact without inference. 135. **Circumstantial evidence** — Evidence from which a fact may be inferred. 136. **Exculpatory evidence** — Evidence tending to show innocence. 137. **Inculpatory evidence** — Evidence tending to show guilt. 138. **Testimony** — Sworn oral statements. 139. **Exhibit** — Document or object admitted as evidence. 140. **Authentication** — Showing that evidence is what it is claimed to be. 141. **Chain of custody** — Record tracking possession of physical evidence. 142. **Privilege** — Right to refuse disclosure of certain communications. 143. **Attorney-client privilege** — Protection for confidential lawyer-client communications. 144. **Direct examination** — Questioning of one’s own witness. 145. **Cross-examination** — Questioning by the opposing party. 146. **Leading question** — Question that suggests its own answer. 147. **Impeachment** — Attack on a witness’s credibility. 148. **Perjury** — Knowingly false material statement under oath. 149. **Burden of proof** — Obligation to prove disputed facts. 150. **Preponderance of the evidence** — Civil standard: more likely true than not. 151. **Clear and convincing evidence** — Intermediate standard requiring firm belief. 152. **Opening statement** — Lawyers’ preview of the evidence. 153. **Closing argument** — Lawyers’ final persuasion. 154. **Bench trial** — Trial decided by a judge without a jury. 155. **Mistrial** — Trial ended without a valid verdict. ### 8. Jury Process 156. **Voir dire** — Questioning of prospective jurors. 157. **Challenge for cause** — Request to remove a juror for bias or inability. 158. **Peremptory challenge** — Limited right to strike a juror without stating a reason. 159. **Batson challenge** — Objection to discriminatory use of a peremptory challenge. 160. **Jury instructions** — Judge’s explanation of the law the jury must apply. 161. **Deliberation** — Jury’s private discussion to reach a decision. 162. **Verdict** — Formal decision of the jury (or judge in a bench trial). 163. **Hung jury** — Jury that cannot reach the required agreement. 164. **Sequestration** — Isolating the jury from outside influence. ### 9. Constitutional & Public Law 165. **Judicial review** — Courts’ power to declare government action unconstitutional. 166. **Separation of powers** — Division of authority among the three branches. 167. **Federalism** — Division of power between federal and state governments. 168. **Commerce Clause** — Congressional power over interstate commerce. 169. **Equal Protection** — Requirement that similarly situated people be treated alike. 170. **First Amendment** — Protects speech, religion, press, assembly, and petition. 171. **Fourth Amendment** — Protects against unreasonable searches and seizures. 172. **Fifth Amendment** — Includes due process, self-incrimination, and double jeopardy protections. 173. **Sixth Amendment** — Criminal trial rights including counsel and confrontation. 174. **Eighth Amendment** — Bars excessive bail, fines, and cruel and unusual punishment. 175. **Fourteenth Amendment** — Applies due process and equal protection to the states. 176. **Administrative agency** — Government body that implements statutes and issues rules. 177. **Administrative law judge** — Official who decides agency disputes. ### 10. Property, Estates & Core Remedies 178. **Real property** — Land and things permanently attached to it. 179. **Personal property** — Movable property and many intangible rights. 180. **Title** — Legal ownership. 181. **Deed** — Document transferring real-property interest. 182. **Fee simple** — Complete ownership of land. 183. **Easement** — Right to use another’s land for a specific purpose. 184. **Lien** — Claim against property securing a debt. 185. **Adverse possession** — Acquiring title by long, open, hostile possession. 186. **Will** — Document directing distribution of property at death. 187. **Trust** — Arrangement in which property is held for beneficiaries. 188. **Probate** — Court process for administering an estate. 189. **Injunction** — Court order requiring or prohibiting specific conduct. 190. **Remedy** — Relief a court provides for a legal wrong. 191. **Restitution** — Restoration of a benefit or prevention of unjust enrichment. This is the cleaned, final working body of terms with brief definitions. It prioritizes terms that appear in both law-school foundations and real courtroom / jury settings.
